# Research playbook

Read before using the internet for Calorica product discovery.

## Questions

Research the decision, not only the requested screen:

1. What recurring eating, planning, or recording problem does the user have?
2. What concepts are broader or adjacent: product, portion, recipe, meal, dietary preference, or nutrient target?
3. How do credible products expose the model, and what does each documented capability actually claim?
4. What friction, harm, privacy, accessibility, or data-quality failure would challenge the proposal?
5. What Android/Play/Data safety constraints change the user-visible behavior?
6. What observation would favor a simpler manual flow or no change?

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer repository contracts; official Android/Google Play documentation; primary nutrition/health guidance and peer-reviewed research; first-party product documentation; then clearly labelled anecdotal reviews or forums. External nutrition facts need direct sources and an access date. Marketing support for a feature is not proof of effectiveness.

Do not manufacture clinical results, weight-loss outcomes, prevalence, retention, or target thresholds. State uncertainty and avoid medical advice. Respect that food, body, and weight data can be sensitive and potentially harmful when presented judgmentally.
